## **4. 文献整理**

文献综述应当产生几种不同的想法和证据。首先，它展现了该概念已被赋予的各种概念定义和操作定义的全貌。这些内容将在后续结合含义分析以及具体研究程序的设计进行探讨。但文献也提供了关于研究者概念的描述性信息。这在整个概念化过程中都非常有用，能够指引想象力以构思设想，并对后续研究中将会发现的结果提供预期。

在分析各类研究时，应当对经验性发现（empirical findings）保持追踪。例如，记录该概念在不同总体中所呈现出的取值分布情况：它是普遍存在还是罕见？是正态分布还是偏态分布？它是如何随时间变化的，抑或是高度稳定？它的相关变量有哪些？它是否仅局限于特定情境或群体（如精英阶层），抑或特定历史时期或社会发展阶段（如剧烈变动的时代）？

追踪语境信息（contextual information）的价值，可以从马丁、麦克内利和伊斯卡雷（Martin, McNelly, & Izcaray, 1976）关于不同大众媒体使用之间关系的研究中得到印证。他们根据媒体使用之间的相关性，将找到的研究划分为两组。一些调查报告指出，读报、看电视、看电影和听广播之间存在高相关；此外，这些测量指标中的每一个都与个人的受教育程度和收入水平相关。他们将另一类研究归入第二组：在这些研究中，不同媒体使用之间的相关性较低，且受教育程度和收入与广播、电影或电视的使用并无显著关联。如此划分后，第一组子文献被发现几乎全部由拉丁美洲的农村调查组成，而低相关样本则全都是城市样本，且在地理上并不集中。作者基于这一背景，设计了一项理论检验，即：媒体使用在低端（农村人口）受到社会经济地位的约束；但在典型的城市条件下，对高成本、高要求的媒体使用形式的结构性约束并不那么严格，不同的媒体在某种程度上是可以互相替代的。这一假设获得了支持，实际上修改了他们对“媒体使用”的概念化。

现象的罕见性（rarity）在评估概念性文献时也非常有用。传统的大众媒体效果探讨（Katz & Lazarsfeld, 1955）强调从媒体到意见领袖、再到广大公众的“两级传播”（two-step flow）观念。但新闻扩散并非以这种方式运作；大多数人在大多数时候都是直接从大众媒体获取绝大部分新闻的（Deutschmann & Danielson, 1960）。大众传播理论中对个人影响（personal influence）的强调有所下降，部分原因就在于这些简明直接的经验事实：在某些情况下，它过于罕见，以至于不值得给予特殊的理论关注。另一方面，在欠发达国家，文献仍然表明人际渠道是影响力的主要阵地（Rogers, 1983）。

文献综述的目的是指引概念化。在研究者推进阐释过程的过程中，文献的所有三个组成部分——含义、操作定义和经验性发现——都应当随时准备作为参考。

## **4. Processing the Literature**

The literature review should yield several kinds of ideas and evidence. Primarily, it provides a picture of the variety of conceptual and operational definitions the concept has been given. These will be dealt with later in connection with meaning analysis and the design of actual research procedures. But the literature also provides descriptive information regarding the researcher's concept. This can be useful throughout conceptualization, guiding the imagination in formulating ideas, and providing expectations of what will be found in a later study.

Keep track of empirical findings while analyzing various kinds of studies. Note, for example, the distribution of values the concept seems to take on in different populations. Is it common or rare, normally distributed or skewed? How does it vary over time, or is it highly stable? What are its correlates? Is it limited to certain situations or groups (e.g., elites), or to certain periods in history or societal development (e.g., times of rapid change)?

The value of keeping track of contextual information is illustrated by Martin, McNelly, and Izcaray's (1976) study of relationships between use of different mass media. They divided the studies they found into two groups based on the correlations between media. Some surveys reported high correlations among reading newspapers, watching television, attending films, and listening to radio; further, each of these measures was correlated with the person's education and income. They put into a second group those studies where correlations among use of the different media were low, and in which education and income were not particularly associated with radio, film, or TV use. Divided in this way, the first subliterature turned out to consist almost entirely of rural surveys in Latin America, whereas the low-correlation samples were all urban and were not geographically concentrated. The authors built on this background to devise a test of the theory that media use is constrained by socioeconomic status at the low end (rural populations), but that different media are somewhat interchangeable in the typical urban condition, where structural constraints on expensive and demanding forms of media use are not so stringent. This hypothesis, which was supported, in effect modified their conceptualization of media use.

Rarity of a phenomenon can be useful in evaluating a conceptual literature. Traditional treatments of mass media effects (Katz & Lazarsfeld, 1955) emphasized the idea of a two-step flow from media to opinion leaders and thence to the public at large. But news diffusion does not work this way; most people get most of their news most of the time directly from mass media (Deutschmann & Danielson, 1960). The emphasis on personal influence in mass communication theory has declined, partly on these simple empirical grounds; in some situations it is too rare to merit special theoretical attention. In less developed countries, on the other hand, the literature still points to interpersonal channels as a major locus of influence (Rogers, 1983).

The purpose of the literature review is to guide conceptualization. All three components of the literature—meanings, operational definitions, and empirical findings—should be kept ready for reference as the researcher works through the process of explication.
